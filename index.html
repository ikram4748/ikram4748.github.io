Data Analyst Agent

Ask a database a question in plain English. The agent links the schema, writes SQL, runs it read-only, catches silent mistakes and fixes its own query.

What I built and measured

  • Built a self-correcting text-to-SQL agent: local-embedding schema linking, a two-layer read-only SQL guard, a literal-value check that catches silent zero-result errors, and a bounded fix loop driven by database feedback.

  • Found and fixed a measurement bug in which rate-limit fallback silently mixed three models across benchmark variants.

Literature Review Agents

A team of agents searches arXiv and OpenAlex and writes a short review where every sentence is cited, every finding quotes its source verbatim, and a critic checks the citations.

What I built and measured

  • Built a multi-agent reviewer (planner, searcher, reader, writer, critic, reviser) in which every finding must quote its source abstract word for word, checked in code, and a critic sends unsupported sentences back for revision.
  • Evaluated citation grounding against a single-agent baseline with an independent LLM judge whose sensitivity is validated by swapping citations to wrong papers.
  • Debugged an evaluation judge that looked perfect but was not: it rated 100% of sentences supported yet flagged only 48% of deliberately wrong citations. Traced it to sentences being credited with other sentences' sources, rewrote it as a fact-checker and verified its quoted evidence in code: sensitivity rose to 89%.
  • On 20 held-out questions both systems reached 97% sentence support; multi-agent cited more precisely (87% vs 80%), but the paired 95% CI included zero at 6× the LLM calls. Fixed a judge that passed every sentence: its catch rate for deliberately wrong citations rose from 48% to 90%.

Financial RAG Lab

Which RAG choices actually matter? Chunking, metadata filters, BM25 vs dense vs hybrid, cross-encoder reranking and HyDE, measured on the exact evidence page in 84 company filings.

What I built and measured

  • Built a RAG benchmark on FinanceBench (150 analyst questions, 84 filings, 12,013 pages) comparing 3 chunking strategies, 3 metadata scopes and 5 retrievers (BM25, dense, hybrid RRF, two cross-encoder rerankers) plus HyDE, scored on evidence-page recall with a dev/test split.
  • On 100 held-out questions, dense retrieval with company/year filtering beat BM25, hybrid and cross-encoder reranking; HyDE raised evidence recall@5 from 44% to 60% and answer accuracy from 47% to 61% (+14 points, 95% CI +5 to +23), vs 24% for naive RAG and 84% with the evidence pages given, which showed retrieval was the bottleneck. Answers graded by a separate judge model validated on gold and corrupted answers.
